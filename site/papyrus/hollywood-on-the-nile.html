Hollywood on the Nile: a century of Egyptian cinema

Egypt has made more films than the rest of the Arab world combined, invented Arabic-language movie stardom, and — after a hard few years — just had a box-office comeback. A short history in numbers.

Long before “content” was a global industry, Egypt was the Hollywood of the Arab world. From Studio Misr’s founding in 1935 through the Golden Age of the 1940s to 60s, Egyptian cinema produced the majority of all Arabic-language films ever made — on the order of 4,000 features — and created the region’s first true movie stars (Wikipedia; Britannica).

That cultural weight is still there. Cairo hosts the oldest film festival in the Arab world and Africa’s only top-tier international one. And after the pandemic gutted cinemas everywhere, Egypt’s box office staged a real recovery: local films took roughly 8 of the top 10 slots at home in 2025, with the market climbing back toward pre-COVID levels (Screen Daily).

The economics have a twist worth knowing. The single biggest market for Egyptian films is no longer Egypt — it is Saudi Arabia, where reopened cinemas made the kingdom the top export destination, reportedly earning more than the domestic take (Variety). Egyptian storytelling, it turns out, travels.

There is a nuance the data forces us to flag: measured in US dollars, the domestic box office can look flat or shrinking even while admissions rise — because the falling pound quietly deflates the dollar figure. Same tickets, same crowds, smaller-looking number. It is the currency story again, hiding inside the culture story.
