Six sites in a single year: how Egypt filled the heritage list

Egypt has seven UNESCO World Heritage Sites — and six of them were added in the same year, 1979. The seventh is not a temple at all. A short tour of the country’s official wonders.

When UNESCO’s World Heritage list was young, Egypt made an enormous early entry: in 1979, six Egyptian sites were inscribed at once — the pyramid fields of Memphis (Giza to Dahshur), Ancient Thebes with the Valley of the Kings, the Nubian Monuments from Abu Simbel to Philae, Islamic Cairo, and more. It remains one of the great concentrations of human heritage anywhere.

Two later additions round out the list to seven. The Saint Catherine area in South Sinai, added in 2002, holds the world’s oldest continuously operating Christian monastery, sacred to three faiths at once. And the seventh site is the surprise: Wadi Al-Hitan, the “Valley of the Whales” in Fayoum, added in 2005 — not a monument at all, but a desert full of fossil skeletons that record how whales evolved from land animals into ocean giants.

It is a nice reminder that Egypt’s heritage is deeper and stranger than the postcard pyramids. The same country holds the tomb of Tutankhamun, a thousand-year-old mosque quarter, a Sinai monastery, and a valley of prehistoric whales — layers of time stacked on top of each other. (Source: UNESCO World Heritage Centre.)
