A capital in the desert: Egypt’s megaproject decade

A brand-new capital city. The largest archaeological museum on Earth. Africa’s longest monorail. Two thousand kilometres of high-speed rail. Here is what is actually finished — and what is still a construction site.

For a decade Egypt has been building at a scale that is hard to picture. The honest scorecard, as of 2026, mixes genuinely finished landmarks with things that are still very much under construction — and it is worth keeping the two apart.

Finished and open: the Grand Egyptian Museum beside the pyramids — the largest archaeological museum in the world, home to the complete Tutankhamun collection — was fully inaugurated in November 2025. The Iconic Tower in the New Administrative Capital, at 394 metres, is the tallest building in Africa. The first line of the Cairo Monorail, Africa’s longest, opened to passengers in 2026.

Still being built: the New Administrative Capital is occupied but sparsely — the seat of government has moved there, but the population is a small fraction of its long-term target. The Siemens-built high-speed rail network (around 2,000 km planned) has no passenger section running yet, and the El Dabaa nuclear plant is under construction with its first reactor targeted for later this decade. The big population and completion figures attached to these are targets, not achievements.

It adds up to one of the largest concentrations of construction anywhere on Earth — genuinely impressive, genuinely expensive, and genuinely unfinished. The interesting question for the next few years is not whether Egypt can build these things, but whether people and businesses actually move into them. (Sources: Egypt’s State Information Service, World Nuclear News, museum and rail operators.)
