Made in Egypt.

Things made in Egypt that you can watch being made, buy or try on your trip: where each is made, where you find it and who makes it. Every item names its source and the day we last checked it.

EgyBI takes no payment, commission or gift from any maker or shop on this page, and none of these links earns us anything.

Cairo's workshops

Khayamiya (appliqué tent panels)

Panels of cut cloth stitched by hand onto a backing, made to line tents and now hung as wall pieces.

Made in
The covered Street of the Tentmakers, just south of Bab Zuwayla, Cairo.
Where to find it
The tentmakers' shops on that street, where the stitching is done in the shopfronts.
See it made
Yes: the makers work in their shops.
Maker or seller
Hany Mahmoud (Facebook)

Source: TRC Leiden: Street of the Tentmakers, Cairo. Checked 18 November 2026.

Brass, copper and silver work, and lanterns

Engraved plates, hand-made brass lanterns and silver jewellery, made in small workshops of the old city.

Made in
Al-Muizz Street and the lanes behind Al-Azhar Mosque, Cairo.
Where to find it
Workshops upstairs and behind the shopfronts of Khan el-Khalili and Al-Muizz Street.
See it made
In some workshops, if you ask.
Real or imitation
Many shops sell factory-made pieces that look handmade, so ask the maker how a piece was made. National Geographic, 24 Aug 2023

Source: National Geographic: a guide to Cairo's craft heritage (24 Aug 2023). Checked 18 November 2026.

Mother-of-pearl inlay boxes

Wooden boxes and tables set with pieces of shell in geometric patterns.

Made in
Workshops in the Al-Azhar quarter, Cairo.
Where to find it
Shops of Khan el-Khalili and Al-Muizz Street.
Real or imitation
Cheap boxes often use plastic in place of shell or bone. Frommer's: Egypt, arts and crafts

Source: National Geographic: a guide to Cairo's craft heritage (24 Aug 2023). Checked 18 November 2026.

Hand-blown recycled glass

Cups, vases and lamps blown by hand from recycled glass, in family workshops.

Made in
Workshops near the Qaitbay complex in the City of the Dead, Cairo.
Where to find it
Shops in Khan el-Khalili, which many of the workshops sell through.

Source: Africanews: Egyptian craftsmen preserve century-old glassblowing (7 Sep 2023). Checked 18 November 2026.

Fustat pottery

Pots and tableware thrown on the wheel by Cairo's potters, who were moved into a purpose-built village.

Made in
The Fustat pottery village behind the Mosque of Amr ibn al-As, Old Cairo: 152 workshops, opened in November 2021.
Where to find it
The workshops of the pottery village.

Source: Cairo Governorate: opening of the Fustat pottery village (2021). Checked 18 November 2026.

Recycled rugs and patchwork (APE)

Rugs, quilts and bags woven and stitched by women from cloth-factory offcuts, through a non-profit working since the 1980s.

Made in
Manshiyat Naser, Cairo, by the Association for the Protection of the Environment (APE).
Where to find it
Souq El Fustat in Old Cairo and the Fair Trade Egypt shops.
Maker or seller
Fair Trade Egypt, which sells APE's work

Source: SceneNow: how APE is empowering the women of Mokattam (9 Jul 2022). Checked 18 November 2026.

Around Cairo: Harraniya and Fayoum

Wissa Wassef tapestries

Wool and cotton tapestries woven freehand by village weavers, without a drawing to follow, at an art centre founded for the purpose.

Made in
The Ramses Wissa Wassef Art Centre, Harraniya village, on the Saqqara road, Giza.
Where to find it
At the centre itself, which sells directly. It says it is open daily from 10:00 to 16:00, Mondays by appointment.
See it made
Yes: the centre invites visitors to meet the weavers.
Maker or seller
Ramses Wissa Wassef Art Centre

Source: Ramses Wissa Wassef Art Centre (its own site). Checked 18 November 2026.

Tunis village pottery

Painted pottery from a village school started by the Swiss potter Evelyne Porret, now run by the village's own potters.

Made in
Tunis village, on the edge of Lake Qarun, Fayoum.
Where to find it
The village's studios. The village holds a pottery festival each year, usually in November.
See it made
Yes: almost any workshop offers a one-day class, and your fired pieces are ready about a week later.

Source: Egyptian Streets: things to do in Tunis village, Fayoum (17 Dec 2020). Checked 18 November 2026.

Along the Nile

Luxor alabaster

Vases and bowls carved by hand from Egyptian alabaster (calcite), a trade passed down in West Bank families.

Made in
Workshops in El Qurna, on Luxor's West Bank.
Where to find it
The workshops along the road between the Valley of the Kings and Deir el-Bahari, and the Luxor souk.
See it made
Yes: workshops carve at their entrances.
Real or imitation
Real alabaster lets light through: a thin piece glows when you hold it up to the light. Outlook Traveller, updated 29 Dec 2025
Maker or seller
Hapi for Alabaster, El Qurna (no website or account found)

Source: Outlook Traveller: inside El Qurna's alabaster legacy (updated 29 Dec 2025). Checked 18 November 2026.

Handwoven cloth of Upper Egypt

Made in
Akhmim (Sohag) and Naqada (Qena), among other towns.

Maker or seller
Association of Upper Egypt for Education and Development, which runs the Akhmim centre (no website found)

Source: UNESCO: Egypt's elements on the intangible heritage lists. Checked 18 November 2026.

Shops travellers warn about

These are reports by several travellers, not EgyBI's own findings. Many guided tours stop at shops; you can ask your guide to skip them.

Papyrus "institutes" on tour routes

Many reviewers describe a short demonstration followed by a long sales pitch, and prices that drop by more than half once you haggle. Real papyrus is made from papyrus stalks; cheap copies are printed on banana leaf.

Source: Tripadvisor: Papyrus Institute, Giza, travellers' reviews. Checked 18 November 2026.

Perfume "factories"

Reviewers of one Cairo perfume factory, now closed, describe diluted oils and bottles swapped after paying. The same kind of stop is still sold inside shopping tours.

Source: Tripadvisor: Perfume Factory, Cairo, travellers' reviews. Checked 18 November 2026.

Alabaster "factory" stops in Luxor

Several reviewers report high mark-ups, pressure to buy and guides taking a commission; others report no pressure. Agree the total before paying.

Source: Tripadvisor: Imhotep Alabaster, Luxor, travellers' reviews. Checked 18 November 2026.

Imported souvenirs at Khan el-Khalili

Reviewers say many of the trinkets at the market's entrance stalls are imported; Egyptian handwork is found deeper in, in the workshops and on the Street of the Tentmakers.

Source: Tripadvisor: Khan el-Khalili, travellers' reviews. Checked 18 November 2026.

How we chose

We list a craft or a maker only when a named source describes it: the maker's own site, an official or academic page, or the press. A price or a tip on telling the real thing from an imitation appears only when a source gives it, with that source beside it. Where we found no website or account for a maker, we say so rather than guess one. We link to the makers instead of copying their pictures. Warnings about shops come from several travellers' reports, and say so.
