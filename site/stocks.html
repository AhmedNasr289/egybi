    How to actually read these numbers.

    The widget hands you the figures; it doesn't tell you what they mean. Open each one for what it measures, what “good” and “bad” look like in context, and the mistake people most often make with it. Numbers below are round illustrative examples, not live figures — and none of this is advice about any stock.

    P/E ratio · مكرر الربحية

    Price divided by earnings per share — roughly how many years of today's profit you're paying for one share. A P/E of 10 means the price is ten times annual per-share earnings.

    There is no universal “good” P/E — it only means something next to a peer or the company's own history. A fast-growing tech name (Fawry) will normally carry a much higher P/E than a mature bank (CIB), because buyers pay up for expected growth. Compare a bank to banks, a developer to developers.

    Illustrative: Company A trades at a P/E of 8, its sector averages 12. That's not automatically “cheap” — it can mean the market expects earnings to fall. Cheap and troubled look identical on the P/E alone.

    Most common misread: treating a low P/E as a bargain without asking why it's low.

    P/B ratio · مكرر القيمة الدفترية

    Price divided by book value per share — the market price against the company's net assets on the balance sheet. Especially useful for banks and asset-heavy businesses.

    Below 1.0 means the market values the company at less than its stated net assets — a possible value signal, or a warning that the market doubts those asset values. Banks in Egypt have often traded below book; context and the sector matter more than the raw number.

    Illustrative: a bank at a P/B of 0.7 is priced at 70% of book value. Attractive only if the assets and earnings are sound; a discount can be the market pricing in real risk.

    Most common misread: assuming below-book always means undervalued.

    ROE · العائد على حقوق الملكية

    Return on equity — annual profit as a percentage of shareholders' money. It answers: how efficiently does this company turn the owners' capital into profit?

    Higher is generally better, but read it with P/B and debt: a high ROE propped up by heavy borrowing is riskier than the same ROE earned with little debt. A consistently high ROE across several years says more than one strong year.

    Illustrative: 20% ROE means the company earned 20 piastres of profit for every pound of equity that year. Steady 15–20% over five years beats a one-off spike to 30%.

    Most common misread: cheering a high ROE without checking whether debt inflated it.

    Dividend yield · عائد التوزيعات

    The annual dividend as a percentage of the share price — the cash income you'd collect from payouts, before any price change.

    A moderate, well-covered yield is a sign of a profitable, shareholder-friendly company. A very high yield is often a red flag, not a gift: it usually means the price has fallen on bad news, or the dividend may not be sustainable.

    Illustrative: a 12% yield looks generous — but if profits are shrinking, the company may cut the dividend, and the price drop that produced that yield can dwarf the payout.

    Most common misread: chasing the highest yield on the board.

    EPS trend · اتجاه ربحية السهم

    Earnings per share is the company's profit split across each share. One quarter's EPS barely matters — the trend over several years is what fundamentals are really about.

    Rising EPS year after year is the engine behind most durable share-price gains. In Egypt, also ask whether growth is real or partly inflation and currency effects — a number growing only as fast as prices isn't truly growing.

    Illustrative: EPS of 2.0 → 2.4 → 2.9 → 3.5 over four years is a healthy climb. The same average with wild swings up and down is a very different, riskier story.

    Most common misread: reacting to a single quarter instead of the multi-year direction.

    Evaluate a company in five minutes.

    A beginner's order of operations using only what's on this page. Follow it top to bottom for the stock you picked above — it builds understanding, and it is a learning exercise, never a recommendation to buy or sell.

    1. Start with what it does

      Read the company profile above. If you can't say in one sentence how the company makes money, stop here — never “invest” in a ticker you can't explain.

    2. Size and price it

      In the snapshot, note market cap (how big) and P/E (how the market prices its profit). Big and small, cheap and expensive — you're just placing it on the map.

    3. Check profitability

      In the financials, look at ROE and the EPS trend. Is the company reliably profitable, and is profit per share rising over several years, not just one?

    4. Compare, never in isolation

      Open the screener and line the stock up against two or three peers in the same sector. Every ratio only means something relative to something else.

    5. Confirm at the source

      For anything you'd act on, verify against the company's official EGX disclosures — the widget may be delayed, and the filing is the record of truth.

    The words, explained

    A plain-language glossary.

    The terms on this page, in English and Arabic — what each one actually measures. Understanding beats guessing.

    Market cap · القيمة السوقية

    Share price × number of shares — the whole company's price tag. Tells you the company's size, not whether it's cheap.

    P/E ratio · مكرر الربحية

    Price ÷ earnings per share — roughly how many years of current profit you're paying for. High can mean "expensive" or "growth expected"; low can mean "cheap" or "trouble". Context matters.

    EPS · ربحية السهم

    Earnings per share — the company's profit split across each share. Rising EPS over time is what most fundamentals fuss is about.

    P/B ratio · مكرر القيمة الدفترية

    Price ÷ book value per share. Below 1 means the market prices it under its net assets — a value signal, or a warning.

    Dividend yield · عائد التوزيعات

    Annual dividend ÷ price, as a %. The income you'd earn from payouts. A very high yield can be a red flag, not a gift.

    ROE · العائد على حقوق الملكية

    Return on equity — profit as a % of shareholders' money. How efficiently the company turns capital into profit.

    Volume · حجم التداول

    How many shares changed hands. A big move on big volume means conviction; the same move on thin volume means less.

    Moving average · المتوسط المتحرك

    The average price over the last N days, redrawn daily — it smooths the noise so a trend is easier to see. Not a buy or sell signal on its own.

    RSI · مؤشر القوة النسبية

    A 0–100 gauge of how fast price has risen or fallen recently. Extreme readings describe momentum; they are not instructions.

    How to think about the numbers.

    Reading fundamentals

    What the ratios do — and don't — say

    A low P/E isn't automatically "cheap", and a high dividend isn't automatically "good". Compare a company to its own history and its sector, read several numbers together, and always check the latest official disclosure for context a single ratio hides.

    Indicators

    Indicators describe, they don't decide

    Moving averages show trend, RSI shows momentum, MACD shows the two against each other. They summarise what price has already done — none of them knows the future, and none is a "signal" to act. They are lenses, not orders.

    Risk & DYOR

    Manage risk before chasing return

    Never risk money you need. Diversify. Size positions so one bad call can't sink you. Past performance does not predict the future. Before any real decision, do your own research and consult a licensed financial advisor — this page cannot, and does not, tell you what to do with your money.
