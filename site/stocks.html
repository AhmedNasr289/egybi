The words, explained

A plain-language glossary.

The terms on this page, in English and Arabic — what each one actually measures. Understanding beats guessing.

Market cap · القيمة السوقية

Share price × number of shares — the whole company's price tag. Tells you the company's size, not whether it's cheap.

P/E ratio · مكرر الربحية

Price ÷ earnings per share — roughly how many years of current profit you're paying for. High can mean "expensive" or "growth expected"; low can mean "cheap" or "trouble". Context matters.

EPS · ربحية السهم

Earnings per share — the company's profit split across each share. Rising EPS over time is what most fundamentals fuss is about.

P/B ratio · مكرر القيمة الدفترية

Price ÷ book value per share. Below 1 means the market prices it under its net assets — a value signal, or a warning.

Dividend yield · عائد التوزيعات

Annual dividend ÷ price, as a %. The income you'd earn from payouts. A very high yield can be a red flag, not a gift.

ROE · العائد على حقوق الملكية

Return on equity — profit as a % of shareholders' money. How efficiently the company turns capital into profit.

Volume · حجم التداول

How many shares changed hands. A big move on big volume means conviction; the same move on thin volume means less.

Moving average · المتوسط المتحرك

The average price over the last N days, redrawn daily — it smooths the noise so a trend is easier to see. Not a buy or sell signal on its own.

RSI · مؤشر القوة النسبية

A 0–100 gauge of how fast price has risen or fallen recently. Extreme readings describe momentum; they are not instructions.

How to think about the numbers.

Reading fundamentals

What the ratios do — and don't — say

A low P/E isn't automatically "cheap", and a high dividend isn't automatically "good". Compare a company to its own history and its sector, read several numbers together, and always check the latest official disclosure for context a single ratio hides.

Indicators

Indicators describe, they don't decide

Moving averages show trend, RSI shows momentum, MACD shows the two against each other. They summarise what price has already done — none of them knows the future, and none is a "signal" to act. They are lenses, not orders.

Risk & DYOR

Manage risk before chasing return

Never risk money you need. Diversify. Size positions so one bad call can't sink you. Past performance does not predict the future. Before any real decision, do your own research and consult a licensed financial advisor — this page cannot, and does not, tell you what to do with your money.
