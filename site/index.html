    Two examples

    What “every figure is sourced” looks like in practice.

    Claiming to be careful with data is easy. These two entries are what it costs: one is a count that surprises people, the other is a number two respectable sources disagree about — where the tidy move would be to pick one and say nothing.

    The obelisks

    Thirty-three survive. Fifteen are still in Egypt.

    Eighteen stand abroad, across seven countries — nine of them in Italy alone. The tallest ancient Egyptian obelisk standing anywhere is not in Luxor but in Rome. The largest ever cut never rose at all: it cracked in the Aswan quarry and lies there still, nearly 42 metres of unfinished granite.

    A number two sources disagree on

    Egypt's population is 116 million. Or 107 million.

    The World Bank put it at 116.5 million in 2024. Egypt's own CAPMAS counted about 107 million that November. Neither is wrong — they measure different things, one counting citizens abroad and the other residents at home. We keep both and say so, rather than picking the tidier figure.

    The identity

    Egyptian history is the subject here, not a theme laid over it.

    The guide through the House of Life is Ibi, an ibis-headed scribe’s apprentice after Thoth, who kept the records. The scribe’s job was to write down what was actually counted — including when the count was uncertain.

    Which looks like this

    Pepi II is traditionally credited with a 94-year reign — the longest in recorded history — per the Turin King List. Most Egyptologists today believe the figure was mistranscribed, and favour a reign closer to 64 years.

    Both readings are kept, because the disagreement is part of what is known. Turin King List; Egyptological consensus per Beckerath, Chronologie des pharaonischen Ägypten.
