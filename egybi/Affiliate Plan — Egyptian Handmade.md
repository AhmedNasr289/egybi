## The finding in one paragraph

No public affiliate programme examined both **pays an Egypt-resident publisher on confirmed terms** and **sells goods that can be checked as Egyptian-made**. The programmes that pay reliably sell the least verifiable goods, and the sellers with real proof of origin have no programme. So the craft income has to come from **direct referral deals with Egyptian sellers**, plus **two or three experience programmes**, not from marketplace links. The site's honesty standard becomes the commercial advantage: it is the reason to read egybi rather than a list of Amazon links.

## What the evidence says (6 November 2026)

**Payout to Egypt**
- **Awin** is the only network with published evidence: Egypt ("EG") is on its publisher tax-residency list, and publishers outside SEPA, BACS or ACH are paid through **Payoneer**. Threshold £/€/$20; refundable 1-unit sign-up deposit.
- **impact.com**'s bank-withdrawal form has an Egypt branch field marked "EGP only". Minimum $10; $10/month dormancy fee from month 7.
- Every other programme says nothing about Egypt: GetYourGuide, Viator, Civitatis, Etsy, Amazon.eg, noon, Jumia, CJ, Rakuten, Partnerize, Admitad. Each needs the written answer.
- **PayPal Egypt** withdraws only to an Egypt-issued Visa card or a US bank account, so "PayPal, no minimum" is not a payout route until a small receipt has been tested.
- **Amazon.com** does not offer direct deposit to Egypt (only a gift card or a mailed cheque); treat it as closed for cash.

**Egyptian-made goods**
- **Fair Trade Egypt** has the strongest proof of origin. It states WFTO membership on its own site (the WFTO directory returned 404, so this is unchecked) and names its producer groups: Armant Women, Bashayer, APE (Manshiyat Naser recycling), Hegaza Wood and others. Its limit: it delivers inside Egypt only, from showrooms in Zamalek and Nasr City.
- **Azza Fahmy** is the only Egyptian seller found that ships abroad from its own store (DHL, about 14 working days, £220–£2,100). It runs on Shopify, so a referral app would be cheap to add. Its pages do not say where the jewellery is made.
- **Yadawee** (fair trade, a MADE51 member) and **Siwa Creations** (founder-led, stocked by the Grand Egyptian Museum shop) tell strong stories but sell little online.
- **Etsy** and **Amazon.eg** do list Egyptian sellers, but a shop's location is declared by the seller; it is not evidence of where an item was made.
- **NOVICA** showed no Egyptian artisans. **"Egyptian cotton" brands** fail the test, because the label describes the fibre, not the place of making.

**Craft workshops**
- GetYourGuide's Egypt pottery category held exactly two classes, both at **Fokhara Studio, New Cairo** (from $21). This is the only hands-on craft class bookable through an affiliate platform.
- The best artisan-led class is **Darb 1718's wheel-throwing class in Fustat** (Airbnb, 4.86 from 66), but **Airbnb has no affiliate programme**, so egybi can recommend it without earning from it.
- Programme terms:

- Many "workshops" are shopping stops: papyrus and perfume circuits, "optional shopping experience". The platforms also rank partly by commission: GetYourGuide says so, and Viator marks paid placements "Promoted".

## Six red flags that block a link

1. The itinerary says "optional shopping", "local workshop", "institute", "factory", "school" or "government store".
2. A craft stop under 30 minutes inside a sightseeing day.
3. A very cheap pickup circuit (about $19–25 for papyrus and perfume).
4. No named artisan or studio, or reviews that mention "pushing sales" or kickbacks.
5. "Egyptian" describes the fibre or the motif, not the place of making (Egyptian cotton, pharaonic resin).
6. The programme pays only by PayPal, and the PayPal-to-Egyptian-Visa withdrawal has not been tested.

On the watch list, not promoted: Saqqara carpet "schools". Whether the children at the looms are working or training is contested.

**Disclosure standard:** the strictest of the US and UK rules.
- The FTC's 2023 Endorsement Guides treat an affiliate link as a material connection. "Affiliate link" alone is not enough; "paid link" is.
- The UK ASA requires each link to be identifiable as advertising before the click, and found "affiliate" alone insufficient (the ASOS ruling).
- **Use "Paid link" beside every link, plus a notice above the first link on each page.** A footer-only notice meets neither rule.
- Never present a platform's top pick or its star ratings as egybi's own judgement. GetYourGuide also forbids reusing its ratings.

## Law, tax and money: what to ask an accountant

This is information to take to an accountant, not legal advice.

**Income tax**
- Income earned in Egypt from foreign programmes is taxable in Egypt.
- Registration is due within **30 days of starting** (Law 206/2020, Art. 25). The clock starts with the first commission, not the first large payment.

**The Law 6/2025 simplified regime**
- Applies to turnover up to EGP 20m, and starts at **0.4% of turnover** up to EGP 500k.
- It requires e-invoicing, and you cannot leave it for 5 years.
- It **excludes** businesses earning 90% or more from one or two clients. A publisher paid almost entirely by Awin and GetYourGuide could look like that, so spreading revenue across programmes helps the tax position as well as the risk.

**VAT**
- Mandatory above EGP 500k.
- Services to foreign clients may be zero-rated (ETA Instruction 45/2025).
- Sources disagree on whether freelancers must register from day one.

**US tax forms (W-8BEN)**
- US networks ask for a **W-8BEN**.
- **Correction to the research brief:** a US–Egypt income tax treaty does exist. The IRS lists it, but its terms were not read here.
- Commission for work done in Egypt is generally not US-source income. A US adviser should confirm how each network treats it.

**Money**
- Since the March 2024 float, there is no official/parallel rate gap.
- No Central Bank rule was found that forces individuals to convert incoming USD; ask the receiving bank.

**Antiquities and wildlife**
- Never write "ancient", "original" or "artefact" in product copy.
- Link only to clearly new handmade work (Law 117/1983: Art. 8 bans all antiquities trade; Art. 43(3) punishes passing off a replica as an antiquity).
- Steer readers away from coral, tortoiseshell, ivory and crocodile items (CITES).
- The US–Egypt cultural-property agreement runs to **30 November 2026**; its renewal is unconfirmed.
- "Made in Egypt" is a legal claim under Consumer Protection Law 181/2018, Art. 9, not just a brand claim.

**Data protection**
- The grace period under PDPL 151/2020's executive regulations ended around **1 November 2026**. Whether a small personal site needs a licence is open.
- Any network tracking script needs prior consent from EU and UK visitors.

**Questions for the accountant**
1. Which income-tax category does affiliate commission fall under? Is a commercial-register entry needed?
2. Does Law 6/2025 fit, given the one-or-two-clients exclusion?
3. Must I register for VAT from day one, or only above EGP 500k? Are services to foreign networks zero-rated?
4. What is the status of the reported "Law 150 of 2026" (books, temporary tax card)?
5. Does a personal site need a PDPL licence?
6. How do I treat Payoneer and PayPal receipts and their fees?
