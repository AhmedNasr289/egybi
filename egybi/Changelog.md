## 2026-07-25 — Atlas first run: what broke, and three fixes
- PR #36 merged (squash `e06b63b`) and **run 1 of "Build Atlas geo layers" went red in 36s**. ## 2026-07-25 — Atlas run 2: the feed's real shape, and a second salvage bug
- **Run 2 went red too, but the self-diagnosing parser earned its keep.** It printed the truth instead of a second guess: the UNESCO feed's root is `<query>`, and its **5,923 coordinate-bearing elements are nested `<poi>` sub-points** carrying only `('iso2', 'latitude', 'longitude')`. ## 2026-08-07 — Cairo Street Report Card: six districts, six eras, measured
- **New service at `/streets.html`** — the fifth. Street-network geometry is one of the few things about urban form you can measure *objectively* from open data, so this computes seven metrics across six Cairo districts spanning the khedivial 1860s to the desert new towns of the 2000s: intersection density, dead-end share, street density, mean segment, streets per junction, circuity and **orientation entropy**.
- **The methodology is the feature.** Five traps are handled explicitly and stated on the page: OSM place-name geocoding is unreliable for Egyptian districts (several candidate queries per district, every polygon area-checked, failures named not swallowed); graphs are projected to UTM before any length metric; counts are normalised per km² because 6th of October dwarfs Downtown; drive and walk networks are kept separate because they answer different questions; bearings are computed unprojected and length-weighted.
- **Orientation entropy validated before use.** Against synthetic networks: a perfect grid returns 1.386 (= ln 4), the same grid rotated 45° returns 1.386 with shifted sectors (so it measures regularity, not direction), and a random walk returns 3.565 against a theoretical max of 3.584.
- **Two bugs caught locally that would only have failed on the runner.** `orientation_entropy` needs **scipy**, an *optional* osmnx dependency not installed by default — now pinned in the workflow. And in osmnx 2.x it requires bearings to already be on the graph, raising `KeyError('bearing')` if handed a bare one — the graph is now annotated once and reused for both the entropy and the histogram.
- **Roses instead of thumbnails.** Orientation roses are drawn client-side as SVG from **36 numbers per district**, keeping the payload tiny rather than shipping street geometry — and they answer "what shape is this place" more legibly than a tangle of lines at thumbnail size. Map is tile-free vector Leaflet, same as the Atlas: zero runtime calls to any map service.
- **AI planner's note, kept honest.** One paragraph per district written from the computed metrics *and nothing else*, with the model told not to introduce history, population or policy it wasn't given; every figure shown beside the note so claims can be checked; the panel labelled AI-generated; and no key means **no file and an honest empty state** rather than a stale set.
- **Manual-only by design** — street networks change on construction timescales, and a pointless cron would hammer the free Overpass API. For the same reason each boundary is geocoded **once** and shared between the report-card and access passes rather than looked up twice.

## 2026-08-08 — The pipeline that checks itself, and the ground in cross-section
- **The mean daily maximum was wrong twice, so the pipeline now refuses to publish an unchecked one.** `T2M_MAX` means three different things across NASA POWER's three endpoints: the hottest hour ever recorded in that month (climatology), the hottest hour in one particular month and year (monthly), and that day's maximum (daily). Only the third, averaged over twenty years, is a mean daily maximum. The first two both shipped under that name — Cairo's August at 45 °C and then at 41, against a real figure near 35. `fetch_tourism.py` now uses the daily endpoint and validates the result before writing it.
- **The yardstick is fetched and cited, not remembered.** The first guard hardcoded published normals written down from memory — an unsourced number validating a sourced one, which cannot tell you which of the two is wrong when they disagree. The build now pulls Egypt's official station normals from the WMO's World Weather Information Service (supplied by the Egyptian Meteorological Authority; no key, no account), caches them into `site/data/visit-station-normals.json` so a later run without network still checks against real fetched data, and compares every site-month against the nearest station within 120 km.
- **Two gates, shaped like the failure they exclude.** A single per-cell tolerance conflates reading the wrong variable — which shifts every cell the same way — with a reanalysis differing from a station, which does not. So: mean signed bias within ±2 °C, and at least a third of site-months within 2 °C. Five synthetic cases confirm the pair rejects both mistakes that shipped, rejects a subtle uniform +2.5 offset, and rejects a set that cancels to zero while never landing close.
- **First full run: mean bias +0.15 °C over 168 site-months, 125 of them within 2 °C.** The residuals fall where the physics says: the Cairo cluster and Alexandria about +2 warmer than their stations, the Luxor cluster within half a degree. MERRA-2 carries no irrigation, so over the watered Delta in summer it is drier and hotter than the ground really is; over the desert reaches it matches. Saint Catherine's −3.2 is not a model error at all — it sits at 1 586 m and its nearest published station is on the coast. That pair is kept and labelled rather than dropped: removing the comparison that disagrees most, after seeing that it disagrees, is how a check becomes a formality.
- **The check is published, not merely performed.** `visit.html` gained a *How it was checked* section printing both gate results, all fourteen site-station comparisons, the elevation caveat and the reason the largest gaps cluster. The source states no normal period for any of its six Egyptian stations, so the page says that rather than filling in a plausible-looking range.
- **Terrain cross-sections — the honest version of the drone flight.** Five measured lines: across the valley at Luxor, up the Giza scarp, the climb from the Gulf of Aqaba to Saint Catherine, the depressions west of the Nile, and the river itself from Cairo to Abu Simbel through twenty-three waypoints on the actual course. Up to 300 SRTM samples each, nine requests per build. Findings: the Luxor floodplain is a flat 78–80 m run about 6 km wide between desert on both sides; Giza climbs 11 m → 119 m in 14 km, so the builders chose that ground rather than raising it; Siwa reaches −82 m; the Nile runs 1 040 km at a median of 76 m.
- **Quartiles are published alongside the extremes.** A min and a max describe the two most unusual points on a line and nothing about the rest of it. Straight legs between river waypoints still clip the terrace where the Nile meanders hardest, so the page states that and shows the median instead of hiding the spikes. The waypoint fix cut samples above 200 m from 99-in-200 to 38-in-300.
- **The traverse.** A slider moves along the profile while a marker moves along the line on a tile-free map, with the latitude, longitude and elevation of every point. There is no autoplay, so there is nothing for `prefers-reduced-motion` to suppress — dragging works identically either way and the profile already shows the whole line at once. Verified: reduced motion loses no rows, no chips and no functionality.
- **Verified across the whole site.** 28 pages driven in Chromium — all 14 live pages, both tombstones, and a sample of the generated challenge and story pages: zero non-200s, zero console errors, zero horizontal overflow at 375 px, zero 404s, nothing stuck on "Loading". ## 2026-08-13 — The bump was never reaching the reader

- ⛔ **The blank map came back, and the cache-buster was never the fix it appeared to be.** Measured on the live origin before anything was changed: `/assets/mapkit.js?v=14` carried `Cache-Control: public, max-age=604800`, and `/visit.html` carried **no Cache-Control at all**. The week-long asset cache is correct — every asset is versioned, so changed bytes are a changed URL, and that was verified rather than assumed: all twelve assets have their content commit and their `?v=` bump in the **same commit**, with zero drift.
- ⭐ **The hole was the HTML.** With no policy on it a browser applies **heuristic caching**, roughly a tenth of the age since `Last-Modified`, and holds the page for hours without asking. A held page keeps requesting the **old `?v=`**, which the browser then serves out of that same week-long asset cache. So the bump only ever reached a reader who refetched the HTML — and the HTML was precisely the thing not being refetched. ⚠️ **Every "hard-refresh and it works" report is exactly this**: the refresh bypassed the HTML cache, not the asset cache. That is why bumping looked like a fix twice and lasted neither time.
- ✅ **Fixed in `.htaccess`, verified live.** `no-cache, must-revalidate` on `html|xml|txt|json`; `public, max-age=604800, immutable` on the versioned static assets. `no-cache` does not mean "do not store" — it means "revalidate before use", so the browser still keeps the page and the server still has the chance to answer 304 against the ETag it already sends.
- ⚠️ **Hostinger does not honour `If-None-Match`.** A conditional request carrying the exact ETag with matching `Accept-Encoding` still returns **200, not 304** — almost certainly the `;gz` suffix its mod_deflate appends. So revalidation costs a full ~9 KB gzipped re-download per page load rather than a small conditional response. That is a worse trade than intended and still the right one: guaranteed freshness for 9 KB. Worth recording so a future reader does not misread the 200 as the header having failed to apply, and so nobody spends an afternoon chasing a 304 that this host will not send.
- ⭐ **The site itself was never broken, and proving that came first.** At 1920x1000 — the reporter's own viewport — counting paths **visible inside their container** rather than merely present in the DOM: `visit.html` 73 of 73, `geospatial.html` 69 of 69, `places.html` 55 of 55 with 106 markers, `EGYBI.mapkit` an object on every one, no console errors and no failed requests. **A shared-asset regression would have broken all of them identically.** That is what ruled it out, and checking more than the one page in the screenshot is the move that turned a page-specific hunt into a server-configuration finding.

## 2026-11-07 — A bad exchange rate can no longer reach the page or the history
- **What was wrong:** on 2026-08-22 the rate API returned the Kuwaiti dinar at EGP 114.43. The day before it was 165.07 and the day after 164.88, and the nine other currencies moved 0.1% or less. The prices page published "−30.68%" that day, and the point stayed in `fx-history.json`. The dinar card's trend line is scaled from its lowest to its highest value, so it has drawn a false dip ever since, with its real ±1% movement squashed flat beside it.
- **Why it got in:** `fetch_fx.py` accepted any non-zero rate.
- **The fix, at the source:** each currency's daily change is compared with the median change of all the currencies that day (`out_of_line`, 10 percentage points). ## 2026-11-09 — Page views from 153 pages were never counted
